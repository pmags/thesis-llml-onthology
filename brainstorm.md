## 1. The Fundamental Problem: Flat Similarity ≠ Hierarchical Ontology

Your current approach conflates two distinct tasks:

- **Semantic relatedness** (are these terms related?) — what `get_similarity_with_descriptions` computes
- **Ontological structuring** (what IS the relationship?) — `rdfs:subClassOf`, `rdf:type`

Louvain communities group terms by *cohesion*, but a community like `['Vulcans', 'Surak', 'Kolinar', 'T'Khasi']` is flat — it tells you these belong together but NOT that:

```
Species (rdfs:Class)
  └── Vulcans (rdfs:subClassOf Species)
        └── Spock (rdf:type Vulcans)
```

**The "similarity" and "composition" labels you're extracting in Step 5 are a good start**, but they're being applied *within* communities rather than *across* a hierarchy.

## 3. Addressing Your Specific Issues

### Issue 1: Islands / Lack of Hierarchy

The root cause is that **Louvain communities are peer groups, not hierarchies**. A community of `{Captain, Starfleet, Enterprise, Federation}` doesn't tell you that `Captain` is a *role within* `Starfleet`, or that `Enterprise` is an *instance of* `Starship`.

**Solution**: Replace communities with a tree structure from the start. Your `build_ontology_graph` already creates a `DiGraph` — make this the primary data structure instead of the undirected similarity graph.

### Issue 2: Expensive Similarity Calculations

You're currently doing $n \times m$ LLM calls in `_evaluate_similarity_llm` (each candidate vs. each community member). Two optimizations:

**A. Cache aggressively** — Store all similarity results in a dictionary keyed by `(term_a, term_b)` sorted tuple:

```python
class SimilarityCache:
    def __init__(self):
        self._cache = {}
    
    def get_or_compute(self, agent, term_x, desc_x, term_y, desc_y):
        key = tuple(sorted([term_x, term_y]))
        if key not in self._cache:
            self._cache[key] = agent.get_similarity_with_descriptions(
                term_x=term_x, description_x=desc_x,
                term_y=term_y, description_y=desc_y
            )
        return self._cache[key]
```

**B. Use representative terms** — Instead of comparing against ALL community members, compare against the **centroid/representative** (the term with highest average similarity to others in its group). This reduces $O(n \times m)$ to $O(n)$.

### Issue 3: Community Handling

Communities work well for **discovery** (finding natural clusters), but poorly for **structuring**. Consider a two-phase approach:

1. **Use communities for initial grouping** (your current Steps 1-4 are fine for this)
